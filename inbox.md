- [Reviewer3 (PyPI package)](https://pypi.org/project/reviewer3/)
  Reviewer3 (previously covered in edition 014 as the bioRxiv-integrated AI manuscript review service) now shipping as an installable Python package on PyPI (agent should confirm version, maintainer, and date during curation; PyPI page was not accessible during this inbox add). Productization step from web-service-and-integration into a scriptable, embeddable tool. Reviewer3 uses multi-specialist agents to assess study design, statistical approaches, analytical frameworks, data availability, code documentation, and how limitations are positioned relative to prior literature. Extends the AI-verification thread from Golub/Refine (this inbox above), Google PAT (015), the "Peer Reviewers Should be Allowed to Upload Manuscripts to AI" position paper (018), and the Vancouver Standard consultation (017).

- [ReviewBench: An Extensible Framework for Benchmarking Human and AI Manuscript Review](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.17.719279v1)
  bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.04.17.719279v1, April 2026 (agent should fetch full author list and abstract during curation). An open-source framework for benchmarking human and AI manuscript reviews with a dataset that spans three corpora across three fields: computer science (ICLR 2025, n=1000), social science (Nature Human Behaviour, n=142), and life science (eLife, n=1000). Reviewer3 (this inbox above) is one of the AI systems evaluated inside it. First named benchmark methodology in this space, which matters because most of the AI-in-peer-review claims tracked in editions 013 through 018 have been reported as vendor stats, ICML pilot numbers, or single-lab case studies rather than measured against a shared corpus. Pairs with the Nature Callaway survey (edition 016) as the empirical infrastructure the field's meta-commentary is starting to reference.

- [Benjamin Golub on the OpenAI-president math-slop incident and Refine.ink](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/benjamin-golub-2b481016_in-case-you-think-the-problem-of-science-share-7487161384363794432-X1YM/)
  Benjamin Golub (Refine, refine.ink), LinkedIn July 2026. Uses a concrete recent incident to argue for an automated independent verification layer: OpenAI president Greg Brockman shared a mathematical AI paper on X as proud evidence of the technology's power; within a day mathematician Tony Feng identified a gap that undercut the paper's central argument; the author conceded; Brockman deleted the tweet. Golub's frame: "heroes catching errors one at a time won't hold against the volume of AI-generated research now coming." Refine.ink is his product positioning: automated independent verification layer researchers pass claims through before amplification. Extends the AI-verification-layer thread this newsletter has been tracking (QED Science ranking preprints in edition 016, Reviewer3 on bioRxiv in 013, Google Paper Assistant Tool in 015, data poisoning arXiv paper in 017). Pairs directly with Thorp's Science editorial (edition 017 lead): where Thorp says AI catches errors but requires more human effort not less, Golub says that ratio is already broken and needs an automated layer. Also useful as a concrete-incident anchor for the AI-slop-at-scale problem that the "AI Scientist" cluster (Biomni, Robin, Co-Scientist) implicitly generates.
