- [Tristan Buckmaster's public statement on the OpenAI Navier-Stokes incident (PDF)](https://cims.nyu.edu/~tristanb/statement.pdf)
  Tristan Buckmaster (NYU Courant), PDF hosted at cims.nyu.edu, dated September 8 2026. This is the primary provenance source for the OpenAI Navier-Stokes announcement, distinct from Holtz's analysis and Quanta's news summary (both above in this inbox). Buckmaster narrates what he was told, when, and what was proposed to him, and he quotes verbatim from his email to OpenAI and from the calls that followed. Content beyond the news headlines follows.

  What Buckmaster and Alpöge actually released. Three simultaneous results published today: finite-time blowup with smooth forcing for (1) incompressible porous media, (2) Boussinesq, and (3) 3D incompressible Euler. Also a fourth (hypo-dissipative Navier-Stokes) held back for lack of Lean verification and a presentable writeup. All three published proofs used Anthropic's Claude, OpenAI's Codex with GPT-5.6 Sol, and Astra (Astra only for writeups and auditing). Timeline: slow progress for a year, breakthrough on August 15 (blowup for Boussinesq and Euler under smooth forcing), Lean-verified August 22.

  Attribution and framing. Buckmaster: "The credit for the basic idea of this program goes to Diego Córdoba and Luis Martínez-Zoroa, who for several years have been exploring the construction of forced blow ups." And: "I believe Luis Martínez-Zoroa deserves a Fields Medal." On the moment: "This is a Deep Blue-Kasparov moment. The community needs to have serious and unhurried discussion about where to go from here."

  The incident, in Buckmaster's account. September 3: a rumor was circulating that Anthropic had solved a major open problem, and Alpöge had received tips that information about their work had reached OpenAI. Buckmaster emailed a prominent mathematician at OpenAI (name redacted). The reply that day included "we are always thrilled when mathematician make progress with our models. Additionally if there is anything in terms of compute from OpenAI's end we would be happy to provide it." September 6 (Sunday) at 12:45pm, OpenAI asked to meet "at any point today." Sébastien Bubeck (lead of OpenAI's math team) joined. Alpöge was not on the call. Bubeck had told Alpöge by text that "very little human input" had been used to produce OpenAI's proof. Over the course of the call, as members of Bubeck's team sent him corrections and details over their internal chat, it became clear this was false. An entire team had been working on the problem, work had started on the unforced problem, they had warmed the model up on Euler first, even the prompt shown to Buckmaster had been generated by prompting Codex, and "an insane amount of compute had been used." When asked when OpenAI's first prompt was sent, they would not directly answer for some time. Eventually they agreed "in the past few days, after information about our work had reached OpenAI." When asked whether the model had been trained on or had access to their private Codex sessions (which contained every draft of the project), Buckmaster was told "the model did not look up user data." When he asked again about training, he received no answer.

  Two proposals from OpenAI. (1) Buckmaster and Alpöge post their Euler result, OpenAI posts its Navier-Stokes result the next day. (2) After posting Euler, Buckmaster alone writes a paper presenting the Navier-Stokes result, acknowledging that an OpenAI model resolved it. Bubeck twice asserted he wanted Alpöge removed from authorship, saying "it would all be simple if only it were not the case that, and it was so annoying that, Levent works at Anthropic." OpenAI offered to publicly say Buckmaster and Alpöge deserved the Clay Prize and were "the closest humans to the problem." Buckmaster declined both offers, and said he would go public if OpenAI proceeded as proposed.

  Direct quotes from Bubeck. "Why would you ruin your career?" When Buckmaster asked why an academic going public would ruin his career: "If you don't want me to be nice, then I don't have to be nice." Later Bubeck texted Alpöge separately proposing to talk one-on-one, saying "I don't know if Tristan is being fully rational right now." Alpöge declined and said conversations should go through Buckmaster.

  What Buckmaster is explicit about NOT claiming. "I have not seen OpenAI's proof. I do not know what their model did, or how. I do not know whether our data was used. I am not accusing anyone of anything. I am stating what I was told, when, and what was proposed to me." His closing line: "If indeed an OpenAI model did close the gap to Navier-Stokes, that is a remarkable thing and it should be said loudly, by them, with the history intact."

  Relevance to the beat. This is the raw primary evidence for the biggest AI-in-science story of the year, and it should be treated as a distinct edition item, not folded into a Quanta-derived summary. Also worth noting for editorial framing: the care Buckmaster takes to distinguish "what I was told" from "what I know" is the same care his statement accuses OpenAI of skipping.
