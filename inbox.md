- [Summit on Math Research in the Age of AI: report](https://cmsa.fas.harvard.edu/aimathresearch_summit/)
  32 mathematicians met at Harvard's CMSA on 22 and 23 October (organizers Freed, Guth, Kra, Riehl, Abouzaid) and published recommendations on how AI is changing math research: credit and authorship for AI-assisted work, formalization in Lean as a supplement to the literature, training early-career mathematicians, and new publication formats (interactive articles, databases, venues that separate dissemination from validation). Names Hexagon and Palomar as new venues and the Bourgain Project, Erdős Problems and LMFDB as new ways to organize math. Report PDF: https://cmsa.fas.harvard.edu/media/2026/10/Math-research-summit-report.pdf

- [EMNLP 2026 discussion of AI reviewing and submission volume (Tanise Ceron)](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/taniseceron_emnlp2026-share-7521963028620394496-mHyb/)
  A LinkedIn post by Tanise Ceron reporting on AI-reviewing discussions at EMNLP 2026. It describes an experiment presented there by Desmond Elliott, Mike Zhang and colleagues, in which authors received AI-generated reviews of their own papers and rated them. The reviews were good at summarizing contributions and strengths but poor at finding weaknesses, missing related work and catching technical errors. The post ties the push for AI reviewing to the rise in EMNLP submissions and proposes requiring at least one author with a prior ACL or ML publication. Commenters argue this would shut out interdisciplinary and independent researchers. One notes that the current submission lottery already caps authors without a service record, and that the October cycle drew many out-of-scope submissions. This pairs with the No. 032 preprint on LLMs reviewing published Nature, Science and Cell papers, which also found low flaw detection.
