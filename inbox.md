- [Olga Holtz on OpenAI's claimed Navier-Stokes blow-up proof: verification is not provenance](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/olga-holtz_on-the-navierstokes-millennium-prize-problem-share-7503151528011206656-RKBA/)
  Olga Holtz (mathematician working at the intersection of math and AI, curation agent to confirm affiliation, likely UC Berkeley), LinkedIn approximately September 8 or 9 2026. Long comment on OpenAI's claim to have constructed a proof of finite-time blow-up for the forced three-dimensional incompressible Navier-Stokes equations. Effort reported by OpenAI: about 10,000 concurrent agents, 88 hours of compute, 130 billion tokens, human researcher direction, and formal Lean verification taking 17 hours. Holtz raises three concerns. First, the forced variant is permitted under Clay's Millennium Prize criteria but differs from the unforced problem, which is what most people mean when they say "the Navier-Stokes millennium problem." Second, the proof follows a methodology developed by Diego Córdoba and Luis Martínez Zoroa (Spanish mathematicians), which was not attributed prominently in the OpenAI announcement. Third, Tristan Buckmaster raised concerns that private Codex material might have leaked and shaped the OpenAI system's approach. Holtz's read: "That leakage is absolutely possible, in my view." Key quote from the post: "Proof verification is not provenance verification. It cannot, by itself, settle scientific credit or establish that the discovery process was fair." Relevance to the beat. Every substantive AI-in-science thread of the past two months converges here. AI-in-math benchmarks (the Leipzig result where AI solved 98 of 100 problems, and MathOnChallenge, also in this inbox). AI-scientist product claims (Google Co-Scientist, Anthropic Team plan for scientists, OpenAI Rosalind Workbench, all announced in August 2026). The verification-layer argument (Benjamin Golub's Refine.ink, the Brockman-Feng math slop incident, Reviewer3, ReviewBench). And the Google DeepMind "LLMs Can't Jump" position paper by Tom Zahavy, arguing LLMs handle deduction but not the abductive jump to new premises. The Zahavy argument is directly relevant here: the Córdoba and Martínez Zoroa methodology was the abductive step, and the OpenAI system apparently deployed it deductively. Also 10,000 concurrent agents plus 130 billion tokens on one problem is a compute scale only one lab can afford, which reshapes what "a fair discovery process" even means.
