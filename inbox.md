- [How well does AI peer review work?](https://www.paullitvak.com/p/how-well-does-ai-peer-review-work)
  Paul Litvak, August 10 2026. An empirical test: the author and Claude planted 100 errors (both alterations and deliberate omissions, spread over 62 categories) into 10 open-access psychology papers, then measured how many each AI system caught. Systems tested: Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, plus the commercial tools Reviewer3 and Refine.ink. Results: the best single system (GPT-5.5) caught 71 of 100, the worst (Reviewer3) caught 30, but pooling all of them as an ensemble caught 93 of 100. The 7 that stayed undetectable were all omissions (missing information). The sharp findings: different LLMs are surprisingly uncorrelated in which errors they catch, so ensembles help a lot, and every system struggles with omission-type errors. Caveats: false positives, and whether planted errors match the real distribution of errors in papers. Why it matters: a concrete measurement of AI-as-reviewer, and it names Refine.ink (the verification layer from edition 019's Golub item) and Reviewer3.
