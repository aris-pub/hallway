- [Fast, but incomplete and inaccurate: fifteen AI-powered tools for literature search](https://renebekkers.wordpress.com/2026/10/10/fast-but-incomplete-and-inaccurate-twelve-ai-powered-tools-for-literature-search/)
  René Bekkers (Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam, research on charitable giving) tested AI literature search tools against a known answer. A team of six scholars had found 48 replications of experiments on charitable giving by searching Google Scholar by hand. Bekkers then asked each tool to find them. The best tool, Google Scholar Labs, found about one third. Most tools, including Perplexity, found fewer than 10, and Elsevier's LeapSpace returned false positives for up to 70 percent of its results. All fifteen tools together found half of the 48. Longer prompts with explanations and examples did not improve the results. The tools did find some real replications that the manual search had missed, because those papers used other terms. The post first covered twelve tools on October 10, and Bekkers added three more on November 15 (two of them found one and zero of the 48). The paper, with prompts, data and code, is on OSF (https://osf.io/ghxn3/files/yqaju). Bekkers will present it at the Leibniz Association Open Science day in Berlin and then submit it to Open Research Europe, so it has not been peer reviewed. Found through Bekkers's LinkedIn post (https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rene-bekkers-6ab2162_update-i-tested-three-more-ai-powered-tools-share-7527722379943387136-Z7Sd/), in which Bekkers asks the tool makers to respond. This is a rare test with a known answer, so recall and false positives can both be measured.
