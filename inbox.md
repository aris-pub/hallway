- [Sean Young on Google DeepMind's "LLMs Can't Jump" paper](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/sean-young-312258371_google-deepmind-makes-a-provocative-claim-share-7487744821063757824-p7Ps/)
  Sean Young summarizing (LinkedIn, July 2026) Tom Zahavy's Google DeepMind position paper "LLMs Can't Jump: Why AI Masters the Proof but Misses the Premise" (PhilSci archive, January 27 2026, https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/28024/1/Scientific_Invention_Position_Paper.pdf). Frames scientific reasoning in three modes: deduction (deriving theorems from axioms), induction (extracting patterns from data), abduction (the creative leap to a new explanatory hypothesis when data is scarce). Argues LLMs can execute deduction and induction but are structurally incapable of the abductive jump required to formulate new premises. Uses Einstein and General Relativity as the case study: no amount of faster math on prior observations yields the reframing of space, time, and gravity. Proposes physically-consistent multimodal world models as the sensory grounding path. Note the timing: paper dropped in January 2026 but is being re-surfaced now (Young's post is one day old) as the AI-scientist product wave (Biomni, Co-Scientist, Robin, plus the White House Genesis Mission committing $5B+ to the category in edition 018) crests. Pairs with PNNL Networked Intelligence/Mycelium (018 lead cluster: no, actually the bottleneck is team coordination) as the second "no, actually" from inside institutional AI research, this one saying the bottleneck is a reasoning mode LLMs cannot do. Also useful as counterweight to the Genesis Mission framing: the same industry that just accepted $5B to make AI-driven scientific breakthroughs is publishing a position paper saying its models cannot make the discovery step at all.

- [ReviewBench: An Extensible Framework for Benchmarking Human and AI Manuscript Review](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.17.719279v1)
  bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.04.17.719279v1, April 2026 (agent should fetch full author list and abstract during curation). An open-source framework for benchmarking human and AI manuscript reviews with a dataset that spans three corpora across three fields: computer science (ICLR 2025, n=1000), social science (Nature Human Behaviour, n=142), and life science (eLife, n=1000). Reviewer3 (this inbox above) is one of the AI systems evaluated inside it. First named benchmark methodology in this space, which matters because most of the AI-in-peer-review claims tracked in editions 013 through 018 have been reported as vendor stats, ICML pilot numbers, or single-lab case studies rather than measured against a shared corpus. Pairs with the Nature Callaway survey (edition 016) as the empirical infrastructure the field's meta-commentary is starting to reference.

- [Terence Tao, "Mathematics in the age of AI" (ICM 2026 public lecture)](https://teorth.github.io/tao-web/slides/age-of-ai-icm-2026.pdf)
  Slides from Tao's public lecture at the International Congress of Mathematicians, July 24 2026. He frames AI as a coming crisis in the foundations of mathematical values and practices, analogous to the 1900 to 1930 foundations crisis (Russell, Gödel), and argues the community will emerge stronger once it examines and codifies those foundations. His framing device is an "AI Capability Conjecture" stated as a template full of "some" placeholders, with weak and strong forms, and he stresses that most public evidence for it is not gathered under controlled scientific conditions and is shaped by reporting bias and non-scientific incentives. He points to the First Proof challenge (1stproof.org): in the controlled May 28 2026 batch, 7 of 10 novel research-level problems were solved at publication quality by at least one of four AI harnesses, at 10 to 1000 USD of compute per problem. A Fields medalist treating the AI question as metamathematical and cultural rather than only technical, squarely on the beat.
