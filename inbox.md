- [Summit on Math Research in the Age of AI: report](https://cmsa.fas.harvard.edu/aimathresearch_summit/)
  32 mathematicians met at Harvard's CMSA on 22 and 23 October (organizers Freed, Guth, Kra, Riehl, Abouzaid) and published recommendations on how AI is changing math research: credit and authorship for AI-assisted work, formalization in Lean as a supplement to the literature, training early-career mathematicians, and new publication formats (interactive articles, databases, venues that separate dissemination from validation). Names Hexagon and Palomar as new venues and the Bourgain Project, Erdős Problems and LMFDB as new ways to organize math. Report PDF: https://cmsa.fas.harvard.edu/media/2026/10/Math-research-summit-report.pdf

- [EMNLP 2026 discussion of AI reviewing and submission volume (Tanise Ceron)](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/taniseceron_emnlp2026-share-7521963028620394496-mHyb/)
  A LinkedIn post by Tanise Ceron reporting on AI-reviewing discussions at EMNLP 2026. - [How good is AI at molecular biology?](https://www.benchling.com/blog/how-good-is-ai-at-molecular-biology)
  Benchling (post by co-founder Ashu Singhal, October 30 2026) released BenchBench-Cloning with DNAForge, a benchmark of 65 molecular cloning tasks built with experts from real experiments. It covers two task types: protocol development and construct design. The top model, GPT-6 Astra, solves 30.6 percent, followed by GPT-6.1 Sol at 22.2 percent and Claude Opus 5.5 at 20.5 percent. Models often get individual steps right but fail to combine them, for example leaving out a control or designing primers that do not work together in one reaction. With ten attempts the top model solves 72 percent of protocol tasks, against 27 percent on one attempt. Fits with Terminal-Bench-Science (also in the inbox) as benchmarks built from real research workflows rather than exam questions. Benchling sells lab software, so note it is a vendor benchmark. - [30 Agents, 18 Pipelines, One Glass Box: Verifiable Agentic Science on Public Infrastructure](https://spond.github.io/CV.js/blog/2026-10-28-verifiable-agentic-science.html)
  An interactive explainer by Sergei Kosakovsky Pond for the bioRxiv preprint Baker et al. 2026 (doi 10.64898/2026.10.26.761864). Thirty frontier coding agents (10 each from Claude, Codex and Gemini) were each asked to plan a replication of one published bacterial RNA-seq study. 29 diverged from the paper and produced 18 distinct pipelines, and one common choice of reference genome erased a transcribed toxin gene. The authors then ran agents through UseGalaxy.org and free ACCESS-CI supercomputers, where every input, parameter and output is recorded. Doing so let them audit 48 published analyses. One audit found that a Paper2Agent result had been computed on a 1,000-cell test file bundled in the repository instead of the cited 5,613-cell atlas. Directly relevant to reproducibility of agent-run analyses and to who checks agent output.
