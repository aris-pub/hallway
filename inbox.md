- [How good is AI at molecular biology?](https://www.benchling.com/blog/how-good-is-ai-at-molecular-biology)
  Benchling (post by co-founder Ashu Singhal, October 30 2026) released BenchBench-Cloning with DNAForge, a benchmark of 65 molecular cloning tasks built with experts from real experiments. - [30 Agents, 18 Pipelines, One Glass Box: Verifiable Agentic Science on Public Infrastructure](https://spond.github.io/CV.js/blog/2026-10-28-verifiable-agentic-science.html)
  An interactive explainer by Sergei Kosakovsky Pond for the bioRxiv preprint Baker et al. 2026 (doi 10.64898/2026.10.26.761864). Thirty frontier coding agents (10 each from Claude, Codex and Gemini) were each asked to plan a replication of one published bacterial RNA-seq study. 29 diverged from the paper and produced 18 distinct pipelines, and one common choice of reference genome erased a transcribed toxin gene. The authors then ran agents through UseGalaxy.org and free ACCESS-CI supercomputers, where every input, parameter and output is recorded. Doing so let them audit 48 published analyses. One audit found that a Paper2Agent result had been computed on a 1,000-cell test file bundled in the repository instead of the cited 5,613-cell atlas. Directly relevant to reproducibility of agent-run analyses and to who checks agent output. - [AI, Mathematics, Education, and a public goods problem](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/10/29/ai-mathematics-education-and-a-public-goods-problem/)
  A guest post on Terence Tao's blog (October 29 2026) by Gireeja Ranade, a professor in an EECS department. Ranade argues that society needs many trained mathematicians and scientists, not only the best few, and that AI weakens the incentives that used to produce them. Students tell her they have lost motivation, and fewer of her undergraduate researchers apply to PhD programs. She treats AI-assisted cheating as an early sign of a wider habit of signing off on work nobody fully understands: code that was not read, and eventually published results the authors do not understand. Verified Lean proofs move the problem to checking whether the theorem statements are faithful. She calls for teaching that holds students to the rigorous details rather than only the high-level ideas. Pairs with Terry Lyons on early-career researchers in No. 032 (Academy for the Mathematical Sciences), and with the Harvard CMSA summit report already in the inbox, which also covers training early-career mathematicians. Tao notes the post was converted to the blog format using AI. Curation agent to confirm Ranade's affiliation before naming it.

- [Egoless mathematics in the AI era](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/11/02/egoless-mathematics-in-the-ai-era/)
  A guest post on Terence Tao's blog (November 2 2026) by Karthik C. S., Amit Sahai and Jie Xue. They argue that the anxiety about AI exposes an older problem: when worth is measured by publications, citations and awards, mathematicians become producers of output. Their alternative is "egoless mathematics", humility about where knowledge comes from, on the view that mathematical truths are discovered rather than owned, so the joy of understanding "can be shared without depriving anyone of it". They note that "mathematical statements may be formally correct without being enlightening", and that falling back on creativity or taste only as an edge over machines does not answer the question. Little here on exposition or publishing practice, the essay is about motive. Pairs with Etingof and Gerovitch on the end of the "ownership culture" (below) and with Calegari's post the day before.

- [The mathematical sciences in an AI-enabled world (Academy for the Mathematical Sciences, pre-publication draft)](https://www.acadmathsci.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Mathematical_Sciences_AI_AcadMathsSci_Oct2026_preprint_final.docx.pdf)
  A 55-page draft report dated 28 October 2026 by Christoph Reisinger, Rachel Bearon, Thomas Bloom, Jennifer Gaskell, David Leslie and Zubin Siganporia, covering pure and applied mathematics, statistics, operational research and the mathematics of AI. Central claim: "The bottleneck in mathematical research is shifting from technical work to expert attention and judgement." It gives numbers: arXiv mathematics submissions in September 2026 were 75% above a year earlier, arXiv has since capped submissions at two per submitter per month, leading probability journals have suspended submissions, and one AI lab released over 700 manuscripts in a day from about 4,000 problems attempted. It warns that "human-digested" mathematics may become a small part of the body of knowledge, with results "sitting in repositories without the coherence and theory-building that aid understanding". Recommendations include assessing correctness, significance and understanding separately, a separate dissemination route for results with AI proofs, and crediting exposition, formalisation, curation and refereeing. Tao linked it on October 31. The UK counterpart to the Harvard CMSA summit report already in the inbox. Curation agent to check whether a final version has replaced the draft.

- [Mathematics has changed. Let's act like it (Frank Calegari)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/11/01/mathematics-has-changed-lets-act-like-it/)
  A guest post on Terence Tao's blog (November 1 2026) by Frank Calegari. The most concrete of the recent essays on what the profession should change. - [The World Is Something We Make (Ben Recht, arg min)](https://www.argmin.net/p/the-world-is-something-we-make)
  Ben Recht (October 26 2026), from a talk he has been giving, argues for fully open language models: open weights, code, data and process. Following Donoho, he credits past AI progress to "frictionless reproducibility" (shared tasks, code, benchmarks, open competition) and argues the field went closed with large language models. He names Marin and AI2 as the only fully open efforts he knows, says compute is the hard constraint, and proposes pooling donated and idle compute. He expects that once an open model matches a leading closed one, the community will quickly improve training, fine-tuning and auditing. - [Mathematics of the Future (Pavel Etingof and Slava Gerovitch, MIT)](https://math.mit.edu/~etingof/futuremath.pdf)
  A 22-page essay dated October 5 2026. They argue AI will move the profession away from an "ownership culture" of priority and publication counts toward "intellectual agency": understanding, judgement, communication and stewardship of a shared culture. Concrete proposals: hiring and graduate education based on talks, discussion, oral exams and collaborative work rather than papers alone. A research model where "a group of mathematicians could spend a term understanding a new body of ideas", whose achievement is "to make a previously inaccessible part of mathematics intelligible to a human community". A "big cleanup" of the literature with AI checking, Lean, and lists of open exposition problems. Publication split between repositories that record statements, dependencies, versions and verification status and articles that "concentrate on explanation and synthesis, guiding readers through a technical record maintained elsewhere", plus recorded talks and interactive companions. Also a section on "AI-vegan" mathematicians who choose not to use AI. Pairs with the Academy report and Calegari above.

- [Why and How to Do Science When AI Is Better at It: A Framework Grounded in Human Understanding and Direction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36116)
  arXiv preprint by Hoda Heidari and Aydin Mohseni (cs.AI, cs.CY, first posted October 26 2026, revised October 30). They argue that how to use AI in research can only be settled after saying why humans should keep doing science, and their answer is "durable, collective human understanding of the world, pursued in service of human ends and values". From that they derive a two-part test for delegating a task to AI: human understanding (the community can still explain, defend and contest the claims, and keeps the skills to do so) and human direction (accountable humans decide which questions matter and what findings mean). They apply it to every CRediT contributor role, including peer review and training. They say it differs from existing guidance by resting on what science is for rather than on integrity norms, and by holding even if AI outperforms people. The general-science version of the argument mathematicians have been making on Tao's blog (Oh on understanding, Calegari, Etingof and Gerovitch, the Academy report in the inbox).
