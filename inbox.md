- [AI helped produce two proofs for the same cryptography problem](https://www.yahoo.com/news/science/articles/ai-helped-produce-two-proofs-160000347.html)
  Scientific American (via Yahoo News), Peter Hall, July 31 2026. Two independent teams used OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra to produce proofs addressing the same open problem in quantum cryptography (unclonable encryption). The AI generated the core proof concepts and constructions; the humans directed it iteratively (Seyoon Ragavan at MIT ran two-hour sessions checking and redirecting; Ananth and Sahai at UCSB/UCLA used a custom system where the AI proposes and critiques, then researchers refined and verified). Named: Seyoon Ragavan (MIT), Yao-Ting Lin (UCSB), Prabhanjan Ananth (UCSB), Amit Sahai (UCLA). Neither paper was peer reviewed before going to arXiv. Pull quote on the culture shift: "Now the general mentality is: if someone mentions an open problem, the first thing is to see if GPT solves it." Caveat raised in the piece: an academic-equity worry that this disadvantages the grad students who traditionally do this work. Why it matters for the beat: another concrete AI-solves-research-math data point in the same wave as OpenAI's Astra "ten advances" (this inbox) and Tao's ICM caution plus First Proof (edition 019), and the not-peer-reviewed detail ties straight to the verification thread.
