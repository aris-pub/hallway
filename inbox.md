- [Reviewer3 (PyPI package)](https://pypi.org/project/reviewer3/)
  Reviewer3 (previously covered in edition 014 as the bioRxiv-integrated AI manuscript review service) now shipping as an installable Python package on PyPI (agent should confirm version, maintainer, and date during curation; PyPI page was not accessible during this inbox add). Productization step from web-service and bioRxiv-integration into a scriptable, embeddable tool. Reviewer3 uses multi-specialist agents to assess study design, statistical approaches, analytical frameworks, data availability, code documentation, and how limitations are positioned relative to prior literature. Related and worth pairing: ReviewBench (bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.04.17.719279v1), an open-source framework for benchmarking human and AI manuscript review with a dataset spanning ICLR 2025 (n=1000), Nature Human Behaviour (n=142), and eLife (n=1000), in which Reviewer3 is one of the systems evaluated. Together the two are the operational + benchmark half of the AI-in-peer-review thread: Reviewer3 productized as installable software, ReviewBench as the framework the field measures such systems against. Extends the AI-verification thread from Golub/Refine (this inbox above), Google PAT (015), the "Peer Reviewers Should be Allowed to Upload Manuscripts to AI" position paper (018), and the Vancouver Standard consultation (017).

- [Benjamin Golub on the OpenAI-president math-slop incident and Refine.ink](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/benjamin-golub-2b481016_in-case-you-think-the-problem-of-science-share-7487161384363794432-X1YM/)
  Benjamin Golub (Refine, refine.ink), LinkedIn July 2026. Uses a concrete recent incident to argue for an automated independent verification layer: OpenAI president Greg Brockman shared a mathematical AI paper on X as proud evidence of the technology's power; within a day mathematician Tony Feng identified a gap that undercut the paper's central argument; the author conceded; Brockman deleted the tweet. Golub's frame: "heroes catching errors one at a time won't hold against the volume of AI-generated research now coming." Refine.ink is his product positioning: automated independent verification layer researchers pass claims through before amplification. Extends the AI-verification-layer thread this newsletter has been tracking (QED Science ranking preprints in edition 016, Reviewer3 on bioRxiv in 013, Google Paper Assistant Tool in 015, data poisoning arXiv paper in 017). Pairs directly with Thorp's Science editorial (edition 017 lead): where Thorp says AI catches errors but requires more human effort not less, Golub says that ratio is already broken and needs an automated layer. Also useful as a concrete-incident anchor for the AI-slop-at-scale problem that the "AI Scientist" cluster (Biomni, Robin, Co-Scientist) implicitly generates.
