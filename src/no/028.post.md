A mathematician at Yonsei University posted this week about what the next generation of paper mills will look like. Unlike traditional paper mills that produce error-filled work, the scenario he describes involves AI systems trained to identify important mathematical problems, solve them, and sell the outputs to researchers who submit with their names on them. Existing detection mechanisms, including AI detectors and plagiarism checkers, do not apply when the work is mathematically correct and has been rewritten after purchase. It is a structural problem that peer review currently has no mechanism to address, and the post calls for community discussion about what institutional changes would help.
