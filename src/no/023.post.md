Google says its AI agent ran autonomous discovery across three scientific domains. A new benchmark of 70 real research workflows puts the best agent at a 30% pass rate. Both can be true, and that's the point.

Terminal-Bench Science is the first benchmark where every task comes from a working researcher's own workflow. Not toy problems, not model-evaluation suites. 70 tasks across five scientific domains. The best agent passes about 30 percent.

That number landed the same week Google published a preprint claiming its Co-Scientist system ran full autonomous research cycles across materials science, biology, and computer science, including driving lab equipment.

Both results are probably accurate. Google's demonstrations were narrow, controlled, and well-resourced. The benchmark tasks come from the messy reality of actual research: parsing unfamiliar data formats, debugging analysis pipelines, working with incomplete documentation.

The gap between curated demonstrations and field conditions matters because institutions are writing AI policies for research right now, and the basis for those decisions should be measurement, not press releases.
