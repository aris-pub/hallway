A symposium at Bergen this week measured what LLMs actually do to research writing. One finding: grammar-only edits (no content changes requested, just prose cleanup) caused a nearly 70 percent increase in neutral essay responses.

Most researchers who use AI for writing say they only use it for polishing. The assumption is that ideas stay intact while sentences get cleaner. But if a grammar-only edit shifts responses toward neutrality by that margin, the editing surface and the content surface are not separate. When a model rephrases a hedged claim into a clean declarative, the claim itself changes.

Every disclosure policy I have seen treats "used for grammar only" as the lowest tier of AI involvement. The Bergen data suggests that tier does not exist.
