The standard failure mode for AI-assisted literature review is confident hallucination: the model cites papers that do not exist. Aaron Clauset's new tool PaperRaven takes a structural approach to this problem rather than trying to make the model more careful.

The model proposes candidates, and before any enter the corpus, code checks each DOI against OpenAlex and Crossref and refuses anything it cannot confirm. Every inclusion and exclusion decision is logged with its reasoning. In a run on cetacean social learning research, 197 papers were proposed, 5 were refused as unfindable, and 158 were kept after 42 rounds of criteria review.

The design treats the LLM as a recall mechanism rather than a source of truth. That framing probably generalizes: in any AI research tool that touches a stable external database, the right architecture separates what the model generates from what the data can confirm.
