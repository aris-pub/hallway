Thousands of parallel AI agents each analyzed one piece of a whole-brain Perturb-seq atlas, finishing in minutes. Blinded human experts preferred their interpretations over a single-agent approach. New bioRxiv preprint. hallway.aris.pub/no/032/

---

A study asked four LLMs to review 200 papers already published in Nature, Science, and Cell. Three quarters of those reviews recommended major revision or rejection. Model-by-model rates ranged from 17.5% to 100%. The variation is the finding: what an LLM recommends depends heavily on which model is used and how the review is prompted, not just on what the paper says. A preprint by Le Guellec and colleagues documents this carefully, testing planted methodological flaws as well, and finding detection rates between 17% and 60% across models. The paper does not say LLMs are useless for review, but it does say their outputs are a low-sensitivity, model-dependent signal that should not substitute for human judgment. This week's edition also covers thousands of parallel agents analyzing a Perturb-seq atlas in minutes, Google's new science tier for Antigravity, and how LLMs reshape citation rhetoric when they draft references at scale. hallway.aris.pub/no/032/li/
