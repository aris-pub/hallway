Tristan Buckmaster is a mathematician at NYU who published a Lean-verified proof of Euler blow-up this week alongside Levent Alpoge. His public statement about what happened in the days before the OpenAI Navier-Stokes announcement is careful in a specific way: he lists what he was told, when he was told it, and what was proposed to him, and he is explicit about not knowing what OpenAI's model actually did.

He is not accusing anyone of misconduct. What he is describing, precisely, is a proposal: write up the result alone, with Alpoge's name removed from the authorship, because Alpoge works at Anthropic. That framing, where credit follows institutional affiliation rather than contribution, is not how academic attribution is supposed to work, and Buckmaster said so.

The rest of this week's edition covers a second allegation involving a different mathematician on a different problem, a declaration signed by 25 Fields Medalists naming the structural version of the same concern, and how Lean formalization became the timestamping tool at the center of the priority dispute.
