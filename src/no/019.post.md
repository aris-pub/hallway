Terence Tao told the International Congress of Mathematicians that AI poses a foundational crisis for the discipline, comparable to Russell and Godel. In the controlled First Proof challenge, 7 of 10 novel research-level math problems were solved by AI at $10-$1,000 of compute per problem. The question is no longer capability.

The same week: Google disbanded the AlphaFold team (Jumper left for Anthropic, remaining staff reassigned to Gemini). Google Research shipped a verification prototype that chains every AI claim to its evidence. A NeurIPS workshop adopted "the bottleneck is verification" as its framing thesis. And the first shared benchmark for comparing human and AI manuscript reviews appeared on bioRxiv.

The field is not debating whether AI belongs in research. It is asking who checks AI's work, and whether the institutions building it are committed to the science or the milestone.
