EditorCheck scanned 512,000 papers from PLOS and MDPI and found more than 10,000 cases where the handling editor had recently coauthored with one of the paper's authors, a conflict-of-interest violation under both journals' policies.

The check was always technically feasible. Author and editor metadata has been public for years. The reason it hadn't been done systematically is that running it across half a million papers required labor and coordination that no single party had reason to organize. Automated tooling removes that friction.

The same situation exists for other integrity checks: undisclosed AI contributions, citation manipulation, paper mill output. The data has been available. The cost of systematic checks at scale is now substantially lower.
